# Pricing a call by its arguments

**Status:** registered 2026-10-13, before any result exists. The criteria,
arms, comparisons and rejection results below were fixed before the change
under test was measured; a result is copied into [Results](#results) with its
run id and never edits this registration. Harness:
[research/experiments/call-grain-args](../../experiments/call-grain-args/).
Temporary workflow: `.github/workflows/tmp-call-grain.yml`, removed after the
run.

## Question

Under `--par` a call offer is kept when its callee's static work reaches 150,000
units, a count that charges every loop a fixed 16 trips
([call-offer-grain](../call-offer-grain/DESIGN.md),
[parallel lowering](../../../design/compiler/parallel-lowering.md)). A callee
whose work depends on the size of its arguments is therefore priced the same for
3 elements and for 1,000,000, and a callee that loops over a range loses its
offer at every size. Snowghost's style interning is such a callee: its ledger
omits the offer at static work 35,791, and the interning stays sequential on
pages of 179,471 elements.

The change under test prices each offered call by its callee's work summary
bound to the call's actual arguments (the same summaries range splits already
bind), decides a constant price at compile time and a size-dependent price at
run time against the same 150,000 unit, and runs the sequential clone from a
function's entry when every offer it reaches is cheap. Does it make a call that
loops over a large range scale with workers, without making any small call, any
formal kernel, or the one-worker path slower?

## The minimal example

`pair.wf` is generated by `generate.py` and sized by `run.py calibrate`.
`workload` calls `mix_words` twice in adjacent statements over two disjoint heap
arrays of `extent` words, `repetitions` times. The two calls are permitted to
overlap (PAR-1). `mix_words` loops over its own range and, for each word, runs 4
to 11 rounds (read from the word) of N mixing steps. The compiler prices that
inner loop at 16 trips, so the callee's static work is about 16 times its work
per element; `calibrate` picks N so that this is 2200 +- 10% units per element,
the figure of Snowghost's interning calls (35,791 / 16 = 2,237), measured on
main's ledger line `omitted offer of mix_words (static work W ...)`. A C
reference (`pair_reference.h`) independent of the compiler gives the expected
result of every timed call.

## Arms

All arms compile the same source with the same fixtures; only the compiler or
its flag differs. `base` is main's compiler (the merge base unless a commit is
named), `chg` is this branch's.

| Arm | Compiler | Flag | Runtime of |
|---|---|---|---|
| `seq` | base | none | base |
| `base` | base | `--par` | base |
| `chg` | chg | `--par` | chg |
| `seq-twin`, `base-twin`, `chg-twin` | byte copy of the arm's image | | |

Widths are 1 and 4 for the pair, 1, 2 and 4 for the kernels (the widths of
`tests/performance/compare.sh`); `seq` and `seq-twin` run at width 1. A timed
process of width W is pinned with `taskset` to the first W of the P-core CPUs
2, 4, 6, 8 (never CPU 0; checked against the topology files: each is a
P-core and no two share a core). `WF_SPLIT_WORK` and `WF_SCHED_REPORT` are
unset.

## Workloads and sizes

- Pair cells: `extent` = 3, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1000, 10000, 100000, 1000000.
  `repetitions` is chosen per cell by `run.py size`: three sizing runs of
  the `seq` image scale it to `target_ms` = 1,000 ms per timed call, then
  the C reference is run once for the expected results. One arm-run is one
  process: a warm call of one eighth of the repetitions (excluded from every
  ratio, result still checked) and one timed call (result checked outside the
  interval). At 1,000,000 elements one repetition is 2,000,000 element mixes.
- Kernel cells: the five kernels of `tests/performance` (Mandelbrot, records,
  FIR, quadrature, stencil) with their formal fixtures and oracles, one warm
  call and five timed calls per process; the process value is the median of
  the five. This is the existing compute-regression workload set.

## Protocol

Rounds rotate and reverse the order of cells and, inside a cell, of arm-width
runs (`balanced_order`). Every round runs every arm of every cell once, so a
ratio is formed per round and the statistic is the median of the per-round
ratios with a 95% percentile interval of 10,000 bootstrap medians, seed
20261010. The pilot (4 rounds, no verdict) reports the sizes, the largest
`seq-twin` spread and the even round count at which that spread is projected to
reach 1% (at least 10, at most 30); the main run freezes that count. After the
first attempt, each cell whose state is not `pass` is measured once more with
the same rounds (attempt 2), as in the par-demand experiments.

**Noise rule.** The twin of an arm is a byte copy of its image, measured in the
same rounds. A comparison X/Y has its twin spread s, the larger of
(upper end - 1) and (1 - lower end) of the 95% interval of twin-of-X / X. A
cell is **slower** than its reference when the lower end of its ratio's
interval lies above 1 + s. A cell whose twin spread exceeds 0.03 is **void**:
the cell cannot decide and no criterion is judged on it.

**States and the second attempt.** For "not slower" criteria a cell is `fail`
when slower, otherwise `pass`. For "upper end at most b" criteria it is `pass`
when the interval's upper end is at most b, `fail` when its lower end is above
b, else `unresolved`. A first `pass` stands. Otherwise the second attempt
decides: `pass` there makes the cell `inconclusive` (the attempts disagree),
`fail` makes it `fail`, anything else stays `unresolved` (after a first `fail`:
`inconclusive`). A criterion holds only when every one of its cells is `pass`.

## Criteria (owner-registered 2026-10-13, all on the 14900K)

As registered, verbatim:

> 1. Minimal example (two concurrently permitted calls each looping over its own
>    range; the board item's mix_words shape but with per-element work about 2200
>    static units, like Snowghost's interning) at 1,000,000 elements: 4 threads
>    take at most 0.6x the 1-thread time.
> 2. Element counts 3, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1k, 10k, 100k, 1M: at every cell the
>    --par 4-thread build is not slower than the sequential build (noise rule:
>    twin of the sequential build; a cell is slower when the 95% interval of the
>    ratio lies above 1 + twin spread).
> 3. Every cell where main's --par build is faster than sequential stays within
>    5% of main's --par time (use the existing compute-regression workload set
>    for this).
> 4. The formal compute kernels' generated code is byte-identical between main
>    and the change (identity check, like 6b's).
> 5. The 1-thread path's machine code is identical to the sequential build's
>    (identity check).
> 6. Snowghost's report no longer omits the interning calls and its style output
>    is byte-identical -- verified by Snowghost on their side.

How each is judged here:

1. **Scaling.** Minimal example (two concurrently permitted calls each looping
   over its own range, per-element work about 2200 static units) at 1,000,000
   elements: 4 threads take at most 0.6x the 1-thread time. Judged as the
   interval of change W4 / change W1 wall time, `pass` when its upper end is
   at most 0.60. Also printed: change W4 / sequential, and the number of
   work hand-outs per call.
2. **Never slower.** Element counts 3, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1k, 10k, 100k, 1M. At every
   cell the `--par` 4-thread build (`chg` W4) is not slower than the sequential
   build (`seq`), by the noise rule above with the twin of the sequential build
   (`seq-twin` / `seq`).
3. **No regression where main wins.** Every cell where main's `--par` build is
   faster than sequential (the upper end of `base` W / `seq` below 1; the cells
   are every pair cell and every compute-regression kernel at each width)
   stays within 5% of main's `--par` time: the upper end of `chg` W / `base` W
   at most 1.05, with the twin `base-twin` W / `base` W voiding a noisy cell. At
   least 3 cells must qualify, else the set is too thin and the criterion is
   void.
4. **Kernel code unchanged.** The formal compute kernels' generated code is
   byte-identical between main and the change: the `--par` LLVM of each of the
   five kernels is `cmp`-identical between the two compilers.
5. **One-thread path unchanged.** The change adds no difference between the
   1-thread path and the sequential build: in the change's `--par` object file,
   every sequential clone (`wf__par_seq_X`, the code a one-worker run executes)
   whose counterpart on main has the same normalized disassembly as `wf_X` in
   the `seq` object has it in the change too, for the pair and the five
   kernels, and `wf_workload` of the pair is required and identical. A clone
   that already differs on main is reported and not judged when the change's
   clone is byte-identical to main's; if the change alters it, it counts as a
   new difference. The owner chose this reading on 2026-10-13 after the smoke
   run found fir's `wf_filter` clone already differing on main, which the
   change does not cause (backlog item par-split-clone-identity).
6. **Snowghost.** Snowghost's report no longer omits the interning calls and
   its style output is byte-identical. External: verified by Snowghost on its
   side; this experiment does not decide it.

## Rejection results

- **R-C1a: not offered.** Criterion 1 fails and the change's calls hand out no
  work at W4 (hand-out count 0): the change does not offer these calls; the
  mechanism as built is rejected.
- **R-C1b: grain is not the cause.** Criterion 1 fails while hand-outs happen:
  the grain is not what limits the speedup (the candidate cause is the
  allocator or the memory path); the change is not rejected on this, an item
  for the cause is filed.
- **R-C2.** Criterion 2 fails at a cell, after the second attempt: the
  estimator is rejected as a floor at that size; the result goes to the owner
  with the cell's crossing so that the coefficient of the estimate can be
  calibrated.
- **R-C3.** Criterion 3 fails with byte-identical LLVM for that cell between
  base and change: the cause is code placement, not the change; rerun with the
  placement control before judging. Failure with different LLVM rejects the
  change's emission for that program.
- **R-C4.** Criterion 4 fails: the change alters a formal kernel; rejected until
  the difference is explained.
- **R-C5.** Criterion 5 fails: the change adds a difference to the one-worker
  path; rejected.

## Void rules

Every verdict is void, and is printed `VERDICT: VOID`, when any of these holds
(`run.py summarize`, preconditions P1 to P10):

- P1 the run is not on an i9-14900K (main and pilot);
- P2 the two compilers have equal binaries or emit equal LLVM for the pair
  (the arms are the same program, or arms were swapped to one compiler);
- P3 main's ledger does not omit the offer of `mix_words`, or the change's
  ledger still does (the example is not the Snowghost shape, or the change does
  not act on it);
- P4 the identity method is not trustworthy: a `--par` compile is not
  deterministic, an output has no `define`, or the control comparison (a
  kernel `--par` against its sequential build) is not reported different;
- P5 the fixtures differ between the revisions;
- P6 a twin is not a byte copy;
- P7 calibration is outside 2200 +- 10% units per element;
- P8 a sequential or one-worker row handed out work;
- P9 main rounds are odd or fewer than 10;
- P10 the identity method does not find the pair's overlapped `wf_workload`
  different from the sequential one in the change object (the criterion 5
  comparison could pass vacuously). Main does not split the pair at all (P3),
  so its `wf_workload` has no overlapped form and its sequential version is
  compared directly;
- a cell whose twin spread exceeds 0.03 (that cell only).

Also: an image or oracle failure, a worker-identity mismatch or a result mismatch
stops the run; no timing replaces a failed check.

## What a wrong result would look like

- Arms swapped or both built by one compiler: P2/P3 void it (equal LLVM; the
  ledger of `base` must omit the offer and the ledger of `chg` must not).
- Identity check vacuous (empty objects, a missing symbol, nothing compared):
  `compare_clones` reports `missing` and fails, a required symbol absent fails,
  `compared` must be positive, P10 requires the overlapped world to compare
  different, and `selftest.py` shows that one changed instruction, one changed
  call target or an empty object is detected while addresses and padding are not.
- A noisy host: a twin spread above 0.03 voids the cell instead of judging it.
- A speedup that is an artifact of checking nothing: every timed call's result
  is compared with the C reference, outside the interval.

## Unverified and limits

- The WF source of `pair.wf` has not been compiled at registration; `smoke`
  is the first compile.
- The static work per element is read from main's ledger as work / 16, the
  estimate Snowghost's figure was derived with; it is not the change's own
  estimate of the callee.
- The identity of criterion 5 compares object files; inlining can differ
  between the two modules for reasons other than the change; a clone that
  already differs on main and is untouched by the change is reported, not
  judged.
- Criterion 2 and 3 are judged on this machine's P-cores with the pinning
  above; they say nothing about other hosts or the hybrid scheduler.

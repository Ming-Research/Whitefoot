Decision: The owning HashMap uses enum buckets and occupancy-only migration into a fresh backing, with one local pending-owner window and the shared exchange helper, because the [matched comparison](https://github.com/mbbill/Whitefoot/blob/20f3b4d618fd64eff245a5b5e7a5162483f94c0b/research/experiments/container-representation/map-library/RESULTS.md#constant-interface-comparison-and-selected-helper-body) preserves must-consume pairs and lowers growth cost without enlarging every bucket, instead of destination planning or per-bucket windows. This accepts two live backings during rehash and dense storage's wide-growth advantage, not a universal layout winner.

Decision: HashMap insertion returns either a payload-free success or one shared pair payload with a replacement/refusal reason, because the [source comparison](https://github.com/mbbill/Whitefoot/blob/20f3b4d618fd64eff245a5b5e7a5162483f94c0b/research/experiments/container-representation/map-library/RESULTS.md#compact-result-boundary-control) establishes a smaller wide result and lower replacement/churn cost, instead of reserving separate storage for mutually exclusive returned pairs. This accepts a second match and a larger zero-sized result; it selects neither compiler union layout nor fewer transfers on every path.

Decision: HashMap probes linearly from a key's home bucket, the top bits of the key's hash after one odd multiplication, over a bucket count that is zero or a power of two of at least two, so each home is a contiguous interval of the hash space and doubling the bucket count splits every interval in two, and a resumable scan whose cursor is a position in that space reports every pair present throughout a pass across insertions, removals, growth, shrinking and rehash, because the [hash-scan study](../../../research/investigations/hash-scan/DESIGN.md#why-a-physical-bucket-cursor-is-insufficient-for-todays-linear-probing) shows that a home of the hash modulo the bucket count lets a rebuild move an unreported pair behind any physical cursor, instead of that modulo home or home-bucket chains. The [second comparison](../../../research/investigations/hash-scan/DESIGN.md#second-comparison-the-link-word-in-the-node) measured chains, even with each link beside its pair, slower than this probing on hits at a million pairs, since a hit reads the chain head and then the pair, and larger at ten thousand pairs; the [next-representation section](../../../research/investigations/hash-scan/DESIGN.md#next-representation-linear-probing-with-high-bit-homes) gives the scan's coverage argument for probing.

Decision: Capacity counts the pairs a map holds before an insertion rebuilds it, three quarters of its buckets rounded up and at most its ceiling, and the ceiling bounds the pairs a map holds, because power-of-two buckets cannot match an arbitrary capacity or ceiling, instead of counting buckets, which would round every reservation up to a power of two and let growth pass the ceiling. A reservation takes the least power-of-two bucket count whose three quarters reach it.

Decision: HashMap rebuilds before an insertion once its filled buckets and the buckets removals vacated number its capacity or more, at the same bucket count when its filled buckets are fewer than half that capacity, rounded down, and otherwise at twice the buckets, or at two buckets from none, while its capacity is below its ceiling, because linear probing walks every filled and vacated bucket between a key's home and its place, a walk that grows without bound as the table fills, and [firn's `INCR`](../../../research/investigations/firn/DESIGN.md#the-hash-maps-growth-results) at depth 16 ran 1.87 times as fast as with a keyspace grown only when full, in medians of three rounds, instead of rebuilding only when no bucket is left. A map reserved to a capacity below its ceiling therefore rebuilds before that many pairs fill it. The three-quarter mark and the half are provisional, since no other value was measured; reopen them when a program's probe walks or rebuild costs point to another.

Decision: An insertion never rebuilds a map whose capacity has reached its ceiling, because the map then has the buckets for the most pairs its program allows and an insertion there allocates nothing, instead of rebuilding it at the same capacity as below the ceiling; its vacated buckets remain until the program calls `hash_map_rehash` or a removal shrinks it. Removal may shrink even a map reserved to its ceiling, and later insertions grow it again, because the [sparse-backing problem](../../../research/investigations/hash-shrink/DESIGN.md#which-table-actually-matters) also affects those maps, instead of promising that a map created at its ceiling never allocates again. The insertion choice is provisional; reopen it with a program whose map removes and inserts at its ceiling.

Decision: A successful removal releases every bucket when no pair remains, and otherwise rebuilds the map once eight times the remaining pairs is below its bucket count, into the least power of two that is at least eight buckets and four buckets per remaining pair, because the [hysteresis argument](../../../research/investigations/hash-shrink/DESIGN.md#hysteresis-and-amortized-work) bounds a scan's empty buckets by eight per remaining pair and keeps shrinking and growth apart, instead of leaving sparse backing indefinitely or requiring each caller to add a shrink policy. A removal of an absent key does not shrink. Rounding up to a power of two follows from the bucket counts above and leaves at most eight buckets per pair right after a shrink, so the next removal does not shrink again. The eighth and the four are provisional; reopen them if the registered grow-only or removal-tail performance criteria fail.

Decision: Shrinking uses the same one-shot occupancy-only owning migration as growth, with no persistent migration state and with the old backing released before the removal returns, because the [migration comparison](../../../research/investigations/hash-shrink/DESIGN.md#one-shot-versus-incremental-migration) preserves the existing bucket interface and ordinary insertion path, instead of adding dual-backing lookup and mutation rules. This provisionally accepts two live backings and a complete migration during the triggering removal; a repeatable threshold-crossing or full-drain p99 regression reopens the choice.

Decision: A scan's limit counts home buckets and a call lends every pair whose home it covers, walking from its first home to the first vacant bucket after its last and hashing only the pairs of the runs at either end to drop those homed outside it, so one call can walk and lend more than limit pairs when keys share a home or crowd adjacent homes, because Redis 7.2.16's COUNT is the same hint and the [study](../../../research/investigations/hash-scan/DESIGN.md#redis-7216-compatibility-and-the-meaning-of-bounded-work) found its scan tests ask no stricter bound, instead of a cap on pairs, which needs the stable-identifier directory or per-scan state. The [hold criterion](../../../research/investigations/hash-scan/DESIGN.md#hold-criterion-set-before-measurement), set before measurement, decides whether the hint suffices; failing it reopens the cap.

Rejected:
- Home-bucket chains over a slot array with a free list, the representation measured first: rejected because a hit reads the chain head and then the pair, two dependent reads where probing reads one run, and the second comparison measured hits at a million pairs 1.19 times and removals 1.05 to 1.12 times probing's time, with 67% more retained memory at ten thousand pairs.
- A link array parallel to the slot array of the chains: rejected because a hit or a removal past the first pair of its chain then reads its link on another cache line, and the first comparison measured lookup hits 30% and removals 20% slower than linear probing at a million pairs with that layout.
- Storing each pair's hash in its bucket: rejected because it adds a word to every bucket, which for `u64` pairs makes the map a third larger, while equality of such a key is one comparison and a scan hashes only the pairs at the ends of its walk; a consumer whose hashing is costly can reopen it.
- A stable-identifier directory beside the probing table: rejected because its memory is the table's plus the directory at every size and every insertion and removal updates the directory too; it gives a work cap independent of the hashes, which the hint semantics do not need.
- Buckets of one cache line with hash fingerprints and an ever-full bit, as Valkey 8.1 does: rejected because for inline `u64` pairs a 64-byte bucket holds two pairs beside its metadata, more memory than plain buckets, and fingerprints save only key comparisons, which matter only for costly keys.
- The first pair of each chain inline in the chain head: rejected because every head then reserves a pair, so at ten thousand pairs its heads alone exceed probing's buckets, and removing a head pair moves the next pair of its chain.
- Lending every pair of the walk without hashing the end runs: rejected because a pass over an unchanged map would then lend the pairs of every run that crosses a call boundary twice.
- A physical bucket cursor over linear probing: rejected because a same-capacity rebuild can move an unreported pair behind the cursor.
- Indexing buckets by the low bits of the hash with a reverse-binary cursor, as Valkey does: rejected because the cursor then needs bit-reversal arithmetic across bucket-count changes and hashes whose low bits agree all share one home, while the top bits after one odd multiplication depend on every hash bit and leave the cursor a plain position in hash space.
- Planning destinations and permuting the old backing as the default rebuild: rejected because the direct ordinary protocol preserves must-consume owners with fewer requests and lower measured growth cost; its smaller same-capacity peak remains a reason to reconsider it for a memory-constrained consumer.
- One inline one-slot window in every bucket: rejected because it increases bucket stride without a consistent advantage over the compact enum protocol in the matched rebuild comparison.
- Selecting dense payloads as the universal representation from wide-growth timings: rejected because the separate index and dependent access have different measured costs, and there is no measured application distribution that makes wide growth the governing operation. A concrete consumer can select that tradeoff later.
- Duplicating the exchange inside migration solely to remove its intermediate result: rejected because the constant-interface source control removes that result but does not establish a stable independent timing benefit after accounting for the unchanged C controls; the registered performance criterion was not fully met.
- Keeping separate payload-bearing Replaced and Full variants as the library interface: rejected because their repeated pair storage and inactive-result initialization have a measured wide-value cost that an ordinary shared-payload form avoids without losing an ownership outcome.
- Inferring a compiler-known sparse representation or a universal compact-enum ABI from the result experiment: rejected because changing the public result alone separates this cost from bucket layout, and zero-sized pairs and retained replacement paths still expose tradeoffs.
- Rebuilding only when no bucket is left, the previous growth policy: rejected because an insertion near that point probes almost the whole backing, and the buckets removals vacate, which an insertion reuses only when its probe reaches one, lengthen every later lookup without ever triggering a rebuild.
- Counting only filled buckets toward the growth limit: rejected because a keyspace that removes as often as it inserts, as expiry, `DEL` and popping do, fills with vacated buckets that a lookup must walk while the filled count stays low.
- An explicit shrink operation as the only way to release buckets: rejected because every caller that omits it, at every removal path, keeps walking a sparse backing, and Firn's counted `SPOP` would need it inside its hold anyway.
- Keeping the promise that a map created at its ceiling never allocates again: rejected because such maps suffer the same sparse walks after removals, and exempting them would leave the shrink rule with an exception no program asked for.
- Migrating a shrink incrementally across later operations with two live backings: rejected because every lookup, insertion, removal and scan then needs rules for both backings and a migration position, which adds work to maps that only grow, while a measured repeatable regression of the one-shot migration's removal tail reopens this choice.
- Rebuilding a map at its ceiling at the same capacity when fewer than half the mark are filled, as below it: rejected because it bounds the walk only while fewer than half the mark, three eighths of the buckets, are filled, since a fuller map at its ceiling still fills with vacated buckets, and it gives an insertion at the ceiling a second backing to allocate; clearing vacated buckets without a second backing is the permuting rebuild refused above.

Decision: The measured quantities of one storage, namely the number of filled positions, the capacity, and a ring's head position, are written as reads of the readonly fields the storage's own declaration carries, `r.len`, `r.cap` and `r.head`, read exactly as any other field is read and assignable by no program, the storage operations being the only things that change them and there being no reader form beside the place, because they are fields of a declared type and a reader should not have to learn a second way to read a member, instead of the separate reader forms `len_of(place)`, `cap_of(place)`, `room_of(place)`, and `head_of(place)` that spelled the same quantity as a call.

Decision: These spellings take no name away from a program: `len`, `cap` and `head`, and the four window part names beside them, are selected by the type of the place they follow, so a binding, a parameter, a field of another type, or a label may carry any of those spellings, because which member a selection names is decided by the place's type and never by the spelling alone, so the reading of a selection was never in doubt and needed no protection, while a reservation takes ordinary names such as a list node's next link away from every program that never touches a storage, instead of reserving the measure and part names from every declaration role.

Decision: The named parts of a window, namely the append position at the filled length, the last filled position, all filled positions, and all free positions, are written as members of the storage place and exist only as vocabulary for declared effect rows and for the judgment of whether two accesses overlap, no program forming a reference to a part, reading one, or writing one, because the built-in window operations must state which region of a window they touch in words that an ordinary function declaring the same behavior can also write, while a part is a region of a window rather than a place holding a value, instead of a private effect vocabulary reserved to the built-in operations or ordinary places that a program could name, read, and borrow.

Decision: Two spellings here are named by the language itself and by no declaration, the length of a range reference, whose type is a reference kind rather than a nominal a declaration could hang a field on, and the four part names above, because neither has anything left to be a field of once a storage's own measures are fields of the storage's declaration, so the alternative to language-named vocabulary is not a declaration but an invented nominal for a path step and an invented place for each region, instead of giving a range reference a declared type and each window part a declared member so that the language names nothing of its own.

Decision: Borrowing a measure with no field declaration is refused at the complete path under MSR-1, and the measure is read by value, because the observation supplies a value rather than a borrowable declared field, instead of admitting the borrow by materializing a temporary reference target.

Rejected:
- Reserving the eight spellings `len`, `cap`, `room`, `head`, `next`, `last`, `filled` and `free` from every declaration role, on the ground that a selection such as a storage place followed by `len` would otherwise carry two meanings: rejected because the member a selection names is decided by the type of the place it follows, so the second meaning never existed, while the reservation forbade natural declarations such as a list node's `next` field in programs that hold no storage at all.
- Writing a measure as a call and a window part as a compiler-private annotation: rejected because the two then share no spelling with the fields beside them, and the part vocabulary would stop a user function from declaring the row of an operation it implements itself.

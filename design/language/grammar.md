Decision: The grammar is deterministic, unambiguous, and context-free, recognized by a generated strong LL(2) parser whose lexer decides a terminal from token shape and exact spelling alone and whose every choice point resolves within two tokens, with no desugaring between the parse tree and the checked tree, because the language is meant to be as simple as it can be and to compile fast, and a grammar made richer for the comfort of a human writer buys nothing here, instead of unbounded-lookahead recursive descent, PEG, GLR, LALR tables, Pratt parsing, or keyword-priority lexing.


Decision: Syntax diagnostics run lexical formation, terminal membership and grammar derivation independently for each segment beginning at a column-zero top-level declaration keyword, or a graph-record row start, report one defect per segment in source order, and admit canonical rendering and semantics only after every segment derives, because independent declaration boundaries expose several actionable defects in one compile while preserving a unique rejection within each segment, instead of full-text recovery or one rejection per compile. Graph segments use a diagnostic-only generated grammar entry and complete graphs retain their ordinary acceptance start, because row diagnostics then share the same attribution and continuation machinery while complete graphs still require the declared row order and at least one module.

Decision: The diagnostic machine computes expected terminals from the continuation of the production instances actually on its parse stack, while recognition retains the generated strong-LL(2) grammar, because a writer needs only tokens that can occur at the failing position and grammar-wide caller unions include impossible repairs, instead of grammar-wide FOLLOW sets for diagnostic frontiers.

Rejected:
- Full-text recovery that inserts or skips tokens anywhere, as in rustc-style recovery: rejected because recovery produces cascaded false errors and needs a separate recovery rule set.
- Keeping one error per compile: rejected because an agent fixes one error per round; the owner-reported Whitefoot-kit baseline rejected 120 of 120 compiles over eight rounds.
- Grammar-wide FOLLOW lists for diagnostic expectations: rejected because they list tokens impossible at the position, such as `=`, `;`, and `)` after a function's single result type, and the owner's writer trial observed agents deleting valid tokens in response.

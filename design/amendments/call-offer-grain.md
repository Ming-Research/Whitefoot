Node: compiler/parallel-lowering

Decision: Under --par, a permitted statement-group call is offered only when its callee belongs to or reaches a cyclic call component, whose offer depth the recursion budget governs, or when the callee's static work summary (the three-round weighted instruction count range pricing already computes) reaches the 150,000-unit work unit a range chunk must reach; every other member runs as the ordinary call its refused edge already makes, `--par-call-grain off` offers every permitted call, and `--par-ledger` names each omitted offer with its callee's work, because on the first large real program 99 percent of call offers called a callee of static weight at most 100 and made page setup up to 37 times slower at four workers than at one, while this rule kept every measured page's two- and four-worker setup within its one-worker time and left the formal kernels byte-identical ([call-offer grain](../../research/investigations/call-offer-grain/DESIGN.md#implementation-results)), instead of the scalar-leaf limit, which cannot reach callees that take aggregates or branch. The rule is decided after checking from the IR alone, adds nothing to an offer at run time and changes no acceptance, proof or value. This replaces the node's decision beginning "Under --par, scalar leaves with at most 16 nonconstant operations".

Decision: Sequential refusal is off unless asked for, and the call grain is provisional: it treats recursion as unbounded work and prices only statically, so a non-recursive helper whose work is large only through its runtime extents loses its offer, and a cheap call into a recursive component keeps one, because no measured program exercises either case while the rule's measured gains are large, instead of pricing each offer by its arguments at run time, which was better on no measured page; reopen when a measured program loses speedup to either case. This replaces the node's decision beginning "Sequential refusal is off unless asked for".

Rejected:
- The scalar-leaf limit as the default call-offer filter: rejected because the offending callees take aggregates or branch, outside its domain, and no limit value changed the real pages' setup.
- Pricing each call offer by its callee's weight without a recursion exemption, statically or bound to the call's arguments at run time: rejected because both remove adaptive quadrature's recursive offer, and the formal quadrature kernel then runs 2.3 to 2.4 times slower at four workers.
- Offering only a callee that contains or reaches a loop or recursion: rejected because a loop is not work, and loops that usually run zero or one trip keep 72 offer sites; setup stayed 2.9 to 5.8 times slower at four workers.
- Sequential refusal by default: rejected because it acts only on the 0.3 percent of offers refused for a full deque and left setup as slow as offering everything.
- Stopping publication from a call site whose offers do not pay, judged at run time: rejected because setup stayed 1.5 to 2.2 times slower at four workers, it failed the formal regression rule on quadrature and FIR, charged every offer a site lookup, and made which offers are published depend on the run.

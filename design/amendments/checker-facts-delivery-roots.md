Decision: Value-initializer delivery materializes only relations the continuation's closure cannot derive: an edge image leaves out a carrier bound implied by the carrier's bound on Z and the other term's implicit bound, which the join re-forms through Z where another image holds that pair, and a joined bound derivable from the receiver's joined bound on Z and the other term's bound on Z roots no delivery join, a postcondition call's candidate counting as a premise only when the joined bound itself depends on such a call; a literal carrier's given-value equality enters the edge's ordinary closure incrementally, because a literal relates its value to every term of the function, so materializing each consequence tripled the retained proof of the real wfgrep program and a full closure per literal edge slowed its check by 29 percent, as the [implementation measurements](../../research/investigations/writer-lost-facts/DESIGN.md#implementation-measurements) record, instead of materializing every joined consequence under a raised proof-size ceiling or delivering a literal as its equality alone, which drops the edge's own bounds that `let v = d; give v;` delivers.

Rejected:
- Materializing every joined consequence and raising the proof-size ceiling: rejected because the roots then grow with each literal value initializer times the function's terms, a change of cost shape rather than the cost of new facts.
- Delivering a literal carrier as its equality alone, closed with implicit bounds: rejected because it drops the edge's own bounds on other terms and gives an unreachable edge a non-neutral image, both of which the accepted copy spelling keeps.
- A full closure per literal edge that withholds the receiver from the term universe: rejected because the given value adds one equality to an already closed state, which the ordinary closure inserts incrementally, and the receiver's only relations there are implicit ones the edge skips.
